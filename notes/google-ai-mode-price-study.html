Google AI Mode Shows Products 21.6% More Expensive Than Traditional Search

ProductRise ran a 23-day study (Aug 9–31, 2026) comparing Google AI Mode shopping results against traditional Google Search. They tracked 2M+ product listings across 100,000+ search result pages, matching the exact same products on both sides of the same queries taken at the same moment.

The headline finding: the same product, on the same query, on the same day, is 21.6% more expensive in AI Mode than in traditional search.

Key Numbers

  • +21.6% — price premium for matched products appearing in both AI Mode and traditional search
  • +49% — median price gap when comparing all listings on each side ($149 AI Mode vs $100 traditional)
  • 1.28% — product overlap between the two search modes for the same query
  • 38.1% — how often matched products show any price discrepancy; when they do, AI Mode is pricier 68.4% of the time
  • 49.6% — the rate at which the main seller differs between AI Mode and traditional search for the same product
  • 3.9 vs 27.8 — average products shown per query in AI Mode vs traditional

What This Means

AI search was supposed to eliminate the friction of comparison shopping — aggregating the best options across dozens of tabs into a single answer. Instead, the data suggests AI Mode curates a narrower, pricier selection. It shows roughly one-seventh as many products as traditional search, and the ones it picks are consistently more expensive.

This isn't necessarily malicious. Google's Shopping Graph has different signals in AI Mode — brand authority, structured data completeness, and seller relationships may weigh differently than the price-first ranking of classic SERPs. But the practical outcome is clear: users who trust AI Mode to find the best deal are likely paying more without ever seeing the cheaper option.

The 49.6% seller divergence is revealing too — for nearly half of matched products, AI Mode surfaces a different retailer entirely, and that retailer's price is higher. The classic open-market comparison is being replaced by a narrower, algorithmically curated path.

For merchants, this is a small win: brands that don't want to compete solely on price may find AI Mode more favorable. For consumers, the advice remains the same as it's always been: comparison shop. Don't trust a single interface to surface the best deal — especially when that interface is AI-generated.
