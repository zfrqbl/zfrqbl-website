The Nvidia CEO Scandal: A Timeline

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive on Thursday, November 12, 2026. The board accepted his resignation as president, CEO, and board member, effective immediately, and named chief financial officer Colette Kress interim CEO (Nvidia press release). The market answered within minutes. Nvidia shares fell as much as 14% at the open, closed down 4.3% at $166.19, and took the company's market value below $4 trillion for the first time since 2025 (Bloomberg). The AI news roundup for November 8-14 put the total value erased at about $1.4 trillion. The windowed numbers vary by source, and the sourced ones are laid out below. From the October 6 record close of $239.24, the stock was more than 30% lower by Thursday's close (Invezz).

The scandal arrived in two waves. The affair reports broke in late October. The espionage allegations followed within a week. A second affair report finished the job, and the board moved on Wednesday night. Each step changed what the board could tolerate. The dates matter, because they show how fast a founder monopoly became a national security file.

The timeline

October 2025. The U.S. Commerce Department and the Singapore government each opened investigations into Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud provider and Nvidia customer, on suspicion it was helping Chinese companies get around American chip export controls. A Commerce official who visited Megaspeed's data center in Malaysia in 2024 found Nvidia equipment still packed in boxes, which raised the concern that the gear was being shipped onward rather than installed as a cloud service (TechCrunch timeline). Nvidia said it had no reason to believe any products had been diverted.

Late 2025. U.S. intelligence briefed President Trump on the surveillance findings about Alice Huang and her relationship with the Nvidia CEO. He did not act on the information (TechCrunch timeline, WIRED).

December 2025 or January 2026. Trump's administration approved sales of Nvidia's more powerful H200 chip to China. The New York Times dates the approval to December 2025; the Associated Press says January. Either way, by the February 25 earnings call, CFO Colette Kress told analysts that only a "small number of H200 products" had been approved and that no revenue had been generated yet (NYT live blog, AP, CNBC).

Spring 2026. A secret memo circulated inside the Trump administration warned that Megaspeed's Alice Huang was a spy for China's Ministry of State Security. Secretary of State Marco Rubio supervised the drafting. The memo stayed secret until the Times reported it (TechCrunch timeline).

May 2026. Rubio warned U.S. officials that Huang was in a close relationship with a Chinese spy. That same month, Trump invited Huang to join him on a state visit to Beijing (NYT live blog).

October 5-6, 2026. Nvidia peaks. It was valued at as much as $5.75 trillion on October 5 (Yahoo Finance) and closed at a record $239.24 on October 6 (Invezz). The stock had risen 1,722% in four years (AP).

October 29, 2026. The New York Times reports that Huang had developed a close relationship with Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, and that U.S. intelligence agencies had concluded she was feeding information to Beijing (TechCrunch timeline). She is no relation to Jensen Huang; Huang is a common surname (AP). Nvidia spokesperson John Rizzo pushed back: "Jensen's interactions with Ms. Huang are not unusual or unexpected. Meetings with customers are part of a CEO's job" (TechCrunch timeline).

November 5, 2026. Three Republican committee chairmen, John Moolenaar of the House China committee, Brian Mast of Foreign Affairs, and Rick Crawford of Intelligence, ask Huang for a closed-door joint briefing. Mast said later that a subpoena would follow if necessary (TechCrunch timeline, AP).

November 6, 2026. The New York Times and the Financial Times report that Alice Huang was a spy for China's Ministry of State Security, recruited to gather information on the Nvidia executive (WIRED). The Chinese government rejects the claims.

November 8, 2026, Sunday. Nvidia's board announces an independent investigation into whether Huang had been "the target of a foreign intelligence effort," while saying it retained confidence in him (WIRED, AFP). Huang sends a companywide letter, also released as a press statement: "I owe my family and all of you an apology. I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did. I am deeply sorry" (TechCrunch timeline). The board hires Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, the firm of Joon H. Kim, a former acting U.S. attorney, to lead the investigation, and Huang faces a separate FBI investigation (Variety). Trump calls Huang "a fine person" and says he thinks Huang should stay on (TechCrunch timeline).

November 9, 2026, Monday. Nvidia shares slide more than 5%. Huang pulls out of speaking engagements at the ElevenLabs Summit and the Anthropic CEO Forum (TechCrunch timeline). Trump says Huang told him he did not know the woman was an intelligence operative (NYT live blog). And the New York Times reports that the Commerce Department's inquiry into Megaspeed was abruptly halted and transferred to the Justice Department after Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, emailed the department about the case (NYT).

November 10, 2026, Tuesday. The Information reports that Huang's relationship with Alice Huang helped facilitate shipments of at least $7.5 billion in Nvidia chips to Chinese firms. Reuters could not independently verify the report (Yahoo Finance).

November 11, 2026, Wednesday. The New York Times, citing five people who worked at Nvidia, reports that Huang had a second affair, with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, who reported directly to him. The two were captured on film "being affectionate" at a hotel during a conference earlier this year. Reached by a Times reporter, Huang said, "I don't think you should be talking to me directly," and hung up (TechCrunch timeline). The Wall Street Journal reports the board is preparing to oust him and is meeting that evening. Stock falls about 8% after hours (TechCrunch timeline, The Verge).

November 12, 2026, Thursday. Huang resigns effective immediately. Kress becomes interim CEO, Harvey C. Jones, a Synopsys veteran on the board since 1993, becomes the first chair in Nvidia's history, and the board starts a search for a permanent CEO (Nvidia press release). Trump recommends Tim Cook, writing that there is "only one man" to take Huang's place and "A Legend replaces a Legend!" (AP). China's foreign ministry rejects the spy allegations. Singapore bans Alice Huang from returning, after opening an investigation into Megaspeed over "suspected false declarations" about server purchases (AP). Senator Tim Scott requested testimony from both Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3, and threatened a subpoena (NYT live blog). Nvidia closes down 4.3% at $166.19, below a $4 trillion market cap (Bloomberg, Variety). Hours after the resignation was announced, Reuters reported that Huang had an intimate relationship with a second, unnamed Nvidia employee, a third affair in the coverage (Reuters, The Verge).

November 13, 2026, Friday. Bloomberg reports that a Chinese company founded by Alice Huang procured smuggled Nvidia chips that were installed at a Shanghai data center with links to Megaspeed; some servers were originally sold to Aperia Cloud Services, an official Nvidia Cloud Partner in Singapore (Bloomberg). Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blames Lutnick's Commerce Department for failing to oversee high-end chip exports. Agencies favored putting Megaspeed on the entity list, he said, "for months... but Commerce has the pen" (BBC). TechCrunch reports that export-banned Nvidia chips sit behind China's most powerful AI models, and that Megaspeed's subsidiary Speedmatrix has been at the center of that trade for years (TechCrunch).

November 15, 2026, Sunday. Megaspeed says it has launched its own internal investigation, calling the reports "assertions... not supported by verifiable evidence" (BBC).

November 17, 2026, Tuesday. Nvidia reports third-quarter fiscal 2027 results, its first earnings without Huang, and they blow past expectations: $115.8 billion in revenue, double a year earlier, net income of $2.70 per share against estimates of $2.48, and a raised forecast for the current quarter to roughly $130 billion. Shares rose about 5% in after-hours trading (AP, NPR). The same call closed the comeback question. Chairman Harvey C. Jones opened by saying the board had "unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO" (TechCrunch). Hours earlier Huang had posted on X that if the board decided his return was in the best interests of shareholders, the company, and America, he would be "honored to answer these calls" — his first public statement since resigning (The Verge). A "Stand with Jensen" petition gained momentum, and Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey, Garry Tan, and Brad Gerstner publicly called for his reinstatement, with Trump saying Huang "learned a big lesson" and that he "would certainly not mind" if Huang returned (AP). The Wall Street Journal reported the SEC is investigating Nvidia, with a focus that includes whether its disclosures misled investors (WSJ). The board formed a national security and export-control oversight committee, chaired by former Goldman Sachs vice chair Suzanne Nora Johnson, and hired search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find the permanent CEO (Reuters, France 24). Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter agreed to answer questions behind closed doors from the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence, and China committees on December 1-2 (NPR).

November 19, 2026, Thursday. California Attorney General Rob Bonta serves Nvidia an investigative subpoena, asking whether its advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public, in violation of California law. "The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags," Bonta said (California DOJ, CNBC).

November 20, 2026, Friday. Reuters reports the SEC's probe is examining potential accounting issues at Nvidia, adding a second focus to the disclosure question (Reuters).

The board investigation

The board's inquiry opened on November 8, four days before the resignation, and its mandate was narrow on paper but broad in practice. It asked whether Huang had been "the target of a foreign intelligence effort" (WIRED). Nvidia hired Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, led by Joon H. Kim, and at the time said Huang would stay on as CEO (Variety). The FBI ran a separate investigation into the relationship. The company has said it had "no reason to believe" that national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but it also said the inquiry would examine all available information (AP).

The board did not finish the investigation before acting. It met Wednesday night, November 11, and accepted Huang's resignation before the independent review had published any finding. The review's findings were still unpublished in the press coverage that followed (Invezz). A source familiar with the matter told WIRED that the alleged relationship with a subordinate, plus Huang's business dealings with Megaspeed, likely played a greater role in the board's decision than the espionage allegations themselves (WIRED). That fits the timeline. The board held its line through the first affair and the spy reports, and moved only after the second affair broke the code-of-conduct question. Nvidia's code of conduct bars employees from situations where personal gain might conflict with obligations to the company, and flags "personal relationships" as a potential conflict (Variety).

The investigation around the company matters as much as the one around Huang. The Commerce Department's Megaspeed inquiry, which had been running since 2025, was halted and transferred to the Justice Department after Nvidia's general counsel Tim Teter reached out about it (NYT). Per Bloomberg's reporting, Teter asked Commerce Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler whether the Trump administration had played a role in detaining Alice Huang (TechCrunch timeline). That sequence, a company lawyer reaching into an active smuggling probe, is the part of this scandal with the longest tail. Congress has two tracks running. The House chairmen's closed-door interview request is set for December 1, and the Senate Banking Committee wants testimony on December 3 (Invezz, NYT live blog). Either can subpoena.

The China broker connection

The $-amount that explains the market reaction is not the $601 billion one-day drop. It is the $12 billion. The Wall Street Journal reviewed documents showing Megaspeed arranged nearly $12 billion worth of Nvidia high-performance chip orders for Chinese companies between March 2024 and June 2025, several hundred thousand chips the U.S. had sought to keep out of China through export controls (The Decoder, WSJ via The Decoder). The buyers were Chinese companies with long-standing ties to local tech giants. Megaspeed got preferential access to scarce chips as an official Nvidia Cloud Partner, with Alibaba as a major client.

The people and the sums align across outlets. Alice Huang became Megaspeed's controlling shareholder in March 2023, per the research group C4ADS (TipRanks). The company bought about $4.8 billion in Nvidia chips over two years and became the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in Southeast Asia (TechCrunch timeline, BBC). Megaspeed says she left her executive role in August 2025 (BBC). Bloomberg reports she has returned to Beijing, with the knowledge of Chinese security officials (CNBC). The Information pegged the flow the relationship helped enable at at least $7.5 billion, through a family network of companies in China and Singapore including Shanghai Century Litong, founded in 2023 and made an Nvidia-preferred cloud partner with priority access to chips (Yahoo Finance, TechCrunch timeline).

The interesting number is the gap between official and estimated China revenue. Nvidia reported $12.4 billion in China revenue over six months, about 7% of total revenue (The Decoder). The short-selling firm Culper Research estimates that more than 20% of Nvidia's revenue last fiscal year came from Chinese customers, and that a significant share flowed through indirect channels. For comparison, Nvidia's direct H20 shipments to China were zero in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, versus $4.6 billion a year earlier, and Nvidia took a $4.5 billion H20 inventory charge (Yahoo Finance). If Culper is anywhere near right, the "China is gone" story Nvidia told investors was incomplete at best.

Singapore's findings are the closest thing to official adjudication so far. Police investigated Alice Huang for suspected involvement in possible offenses related to server purchases, but not for espionage. Her passport was seized and returned in February after authorities concluded there was "insufficient evidence" she committed any offense. They gave her a "stern warning" and barred her from reentering because they suspected she "provided information which she knew to be inaccurate" to downplay her role at Megaspeed (AP). The question of whether servers bought in Singapore went to Malaysia and then on to a final destination, contrary to what was declared, remains open.

None of this is proven against anyone. Megaspeed denies wrongdoing, says it runs a legitimate business, and has opened its own investigation (BBC). Its chairman and CEO, Tan Yong Pong, pledged Thursday to work "constructively and transparently" with the U.S. government (AP). Nvidia has said it had no reason to believe products were diverted. The U.S. Justice Department now holds the federal file. What the documents show is that a company at the heart of the AI supply chain had a distribution channel it could not account for, and that the channel's most visible asset was the CEO's personal relationship.

What happens now

Colette Kress runs the company until a permanent CEO is named. She stays CFO while serving as interim CEO, and the board says it will consider internal and external candidates (Nvidia press release). There was no succession plan to fall back on. Huang ran Nvidia with more than 60 people reporting directly to him, a structure he once described as 60 people who could be world-class CEOs elsewhere, and the company had no obvious number two (NYT live blog, TechCrunch timeline). The names floated so far include Apple's hardware chief Johny Srouji, Nvidia VP Ian Buck, AMD CEO Lisa Su, and Arm CEO Rene Haas. Chris Malachowsky, the co-founder, is also mentioned (The Verge, BBC). Trump wants Tim Cook. Evercore's Mark Lipacis warns of "leadership limbo" because Nvidia lacks "a visible #2 operational or strategic leader" (Invezz).

Chip pricing and product direction now sit with Kress and the board until that search concludes. The third-quarter numbers that arrived on November 17 made the no-Huang case stronger than the bears expected: revenue of $115.8 billion, double a year earlier, against the $108 billion the company had guided, and net income of $2.70 per share beating estimates of $2.48 (AP). Nvidia raised its fourth-quarter forecast to roughly $130 billion, above expectations, and shares rose about 5% after hours (NPR). Gross margin was guided at 74%, down from 75% (Invezz). Kress blamed "extreme pricing conditions in memory" and guided margins to bottom at 71% to 72% in the fourth quarter before settling at 72% to 73% in fiscal 2028. The Vera Rubin platform, Nvidia's next architecture, was supposed to be about 20% of data center revenue in the third quarter, with supply still a bottleneck through at least the end of fiscal 2028. Kress gave a preliminary forecast of about 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028. The forecast survived the leadership change; whether it survives without Huang's pull is now the watched number.

The analyst response has been brutal but not panicked. Dan Ives cut his target from $300 to $225 and called the removal necessary, comparing it to ripping off a band-aid (AP). Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon cut Nvidia to Market Perform with a $200 target and questioned whether any successor could "truly fill Jensen's shoes," though he said the shares screen extremely cheap (Invezz). At least nine firms trimmed targets on Thursday, pulling the average down to about $217 from roughly $343. The bull case is intact on paper. Ives still recommends buying, arguing Nvidia is two to three years ahead of its nearest competitor and the demand trajectory is not changing because Jensen is gone (AP).

The China file is now Kress's problem too, and it is mostly downside. Direct China data center revenue is near zero. Baird's Tristan Gerra lists possible H200 write-down charges among the risks, and lawmakers from both parties are pushing for tougher chip-export curbs after the Huang reports (Invezz). Lutnick is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month, and committees have asked his department to retain all Nvidia records (BBC). The Democrats' letter from Richard Neal and Ron Wyden asks why Lutnick's Commerce Department kept letting Megaspeed buy chips after intelligence flagged the relationship (TechCrunch). Kress herself could face questions about oversight of chip sales, a Jamestown Foundation analyst told Reuters (Invezz). She has already been on calls to reassure customers and suppliers, and sources told Reuters she had pushed back on some of Huang's customer-financing deals (Invezz).

The question the scandal leaves behind

The affair made this a scandal. The governance made it inevitable. Nvidia, a company that controls roughly 90% of the market for the chips the AI boom runs on, was run as a single-player game. It had one founder with 60 direct reports and no succession plan to fall back on. His personal relationships held the supply chain in Asia together, and his judgment defined the product roadmap (WIRED, NYT live blog). That structure produced a $5.75 trillion company. It also produced a CEO whose personal life became a foreign intelligence collection channel, and a distributor network that apparently moved billions in restricted chips while the company told investors its China business was nearly gone.

The fair objection is that the market is pricing the short term correctly. The roadmap is built and the customers are queuing. The demand is real, and the nearest competitor is years behind. Tuesday's numbers were strong, and the stock rose on them (AP). NBC noted the timing is uncomfortable for the sector, with Anthropic's potentially trillion-dollar IPO scheduled for the same week. None of that changes the structural problem. The machine that allocates the world's most important compute was not being watched by anyone who could say no to its founder. The Commerce inquiry that could have caught the Megaspeed channel was redirected after one phone call from the company's lawyer. The board that finally acted did so under the pressure of a second affair report, not earlier, when the spy allegations were already on the table.

The question the scandal leaves behind is not who replaces Jensen Huang. It is who watches the company that replaces him, and whether the AI industry's most important supplier can be run like an ordinary public company, with a board that audits distribution and a real succession bench. And whether executives' personal relationships can stay out of the national security file. Kress's earnings call on November 17 was the first test of whether the machine runs without the man, and it passed: record revenue, a raised forecast, and a higher stock. The board's next CEO pick is the second test. The one after that is whether Washington, which let this sit for a year, builds an export control system that survives the people who run it.
